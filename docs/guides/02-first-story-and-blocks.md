# Your Desk, Your First Story & Building Blocks

*A blank page is easiest to face when your cast, timeline, and outline are already laid out beside you.*

Open almost any word processor and you get one long scrolling sheet of paper. Once your story grows past five pages, you spend half your time scrolling up and down trying to remember what color eyes you gave your detective or what year a war ended.

On your ExoNovel **Desk**, stories work the way writers actually think: in **drafts** paired with **reusable building blocks**.

---

## Step 1: Pick the Format That Fits Today

From your Desk, you can open any of five tailored writing spaces:

- **Novels:** Chaptered books with room for sprawling worlds, casts, and subplots.
- **Articles & Nonfiction:** Structured essays, club features, and research pieces with clean sections.
- **Short Stories:** Tight, focused drafts built around a single hook, turn, and ending.
- **Screenplays & Scripts:** Scene headings, action lines, and dialogue formatted cleanly for film, stage, or video.
- **Poetry:** Stanzas and deliberate line breaks that stay put across every screen.

You can switch your default format anytime under **Preferences → Account & Sovereign Identity**.

---

## Step 2: Break Big Stories Into Building Blocks

Every great story rests on pieces you reach for again and again:

- **Characters:** Backstories, voices, flaws, relationships, and secrets.
- **Plots:** Major beats, turning points, and "what-if" branches you might want to test before rewriting a whole chapter.
- **Timelines:** Dates, travel times, and sequences so nothing contradicts Chapter Two when you reach Chapter Twelve.
- **Themes:** Motifs, questions, and emotional arcs that hold the whole piece together.

Because each block lives as its own credited piece in ExoNovel, you can share a single character or setting with a co-writer—or reuse a town you invented across three separate stories—without untangling a giant shared file.

---

## Step 3: Draft in Any of 29 Languages (Even Two at Once)

Speaking one language in the menus shouldn't stop you from writing dialogue or poems in another.

- Use the **language selector in the bottom footer** to change the app's menus, buttons, and guides across **29 languages** (including right-to-left layouts for **Arabic** and **Hebrew**).
- Inside your **Quick Idea Scratchpad** and composers, use the **Writing Input bar** to switch the language and text direction of what you are writing independently—without changing the rest of your screen.

> **Author's tip:** If you keep switching between the same two or three languages, pin them in **Profile → Languages & Notifications** so your quick switchers stay short and fast.
